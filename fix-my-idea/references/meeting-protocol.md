# Meeting protocol

## Agent orchestration

The facilitator owns the brief, roster, agenda, synthesis, and final plan.
Specialists own their assigned contributions. Use stable seat IDs S01–S12 and
issue IDs R01, R02, etc. Keep a small `STATUS.md` listing phase, completed seats,
missing outputs, and pending exchanges so an interrupted meeting can resume.

For independent openings, prefer fresh contexts with no inherited conversation;
send the complete neutral brief, role remit, and assignment explicitly. Tell agents
not to delegate, inspect unassigned meeting files, or write outside assigned paths.
These instructions are coordination boundaries, not security isolation.

Run only as many jobs as the host supports. Reuse each specialist's own context for
debate and review when practical. If the host cannot retire agents or run twelve
separate contexts, use available workers for multiple clearly separated seat tasks.
Disclose that those seats share a model context and do not describe their openings
as independent. Never silently omit seats to fit concurrency limits.

Suggested assignment:

> You are seat {id}, specializing in {specialty}. Read {brief_path} and the
> assigned phase inputs: {paths}. Your remit is {remit}. Complete {phase} using
> the required format below. Write only {output_path}. Identify assumptions and
> uncertainties; do not invent evidence. Preserve the user's non-negotiables.
> Do not delegate. Report completion or the specific blocker.

Give debate participants the opening record and issue agenda; give final reviewers
the revised idea, decision record, and evidence. They need sufficient context to
challenge the actual proposal, not just the facilitator's favorable summary.

Wait for completion and inspect substantive outputs. A nonempty file is not proof
of a completed assessment. Retry a failed or incomplete assignment once with its
original brief and the specific missing requirements. After a second failure,
mark the seat unavailable. Preserve its gaps in the report; do not fabricate its
contribution or claim a complete 12-specialist review. Continue useful synthesis
with an explicit incomplete-review limitation, unless the missing expertise is
essential to the proposed recommendation.

## Opening format — each seat

- **Perspective:** What this discipline uniquely contributes to this idea.
- **What can work:** Strongest plausible success mechanism and the conditions it needs.
- **What can fail:** Up to three consequential risks: trigger → mechanism → outcome.
- **Evidence:** User facts, verified external facts with sources, assumptions,
  or unknowns. Include confidence and why; do not invent numerical probabilities.
- **Repair:** Specific change for each risk; cost, tradeoff, and dependencies.
- **Test:** Cheapest informative experiment, metric, proposed threshold, and what
  decision each result would change. Explain the threshold's basis.
- **Cross-functional question:** The most important claim another seat must address.

## Debate format — each material issue

1. Record the issue ID, consequence, owner, challenger, and competing claims.
2. Challenger addresses the owner's actual claim, evidence, or proposed repair.
3. Owner responds: accept and modify, reject with reasons, or identify missing evidence.
4. Record the resulting proposal, who bears the cost, dependencies, and residual risk.
5. Mark resolved by evidence, resolved by design, needs test, or unresolved blocker.

Group overlapping issues without dropping distinct failure mechanisms. Ensure
every seat contributes a substantive challenge or response. Prioritize material
disagreement; do not create pointless disputes to fill a quota. If reviewers agree,
ask which shared assumption could make them all wrong.

## Evidence and realism

Research decision-critical external claims when tools are available, especially
current competition, pricing, technical limits, regulation, and market conditions.
Use primary sources where possible and include dates when they affect relevance.
Evidence duties follow the host's browsing and safety requirements.

The facilitator may research once and share an evidence record to avoid duplicated
searches. Preserve conflicting evidence. A source establishes only the claim it
actually supports: market size does not establish demand for this offer, and a
competitor's listed price does not establish willingness to pay.

When research is unavailable, label claims unverified and specify how to check
them. Use ranges and explicit assumptions for costs, demand, and timelines.
Do not invent interviews, customer quotes, traction, survey results, supplier
quotes, or proof that a repair works. Arithmetic projections are scenarios.

## Final review format — each seat

- Status: ready to test / conditional / blocked.
- Which original risks the revision addresses, with issue IDs.
- Which assumptions still require validation.
- Any new risk or inconsistency introduced by the changes.
- Specific release condition or blocker, with the next informative test.

The facilitator checks the assembled plan for contradictions across disciplines:
price versus delivery cost, acquisition versus margin, promises versus capability,
timeline versus dependencies, and safeguards versus the intended user experience.
An unresolved blocker must affect the recommendation even if eleven seats approve.
